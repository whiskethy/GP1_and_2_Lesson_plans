# GP1 Q1 Day 13 — Exploring Unreal Engine

**Date:** Tue 9/15 · **Unit:** 3 · **Chunk:** 1

## Learning Target

Open Unreal Engine, create a first project from a template, and navigate the editor interface.

**Standards:** CSTA 3A-CS-01 (abstractions hide implementation details), 3A-CS-02 (compare levels of abstraction between application, system, and hardware) · ACT WME 402 (technical vocabulary)

**Performance Task Connection:** Students must be able to open UE and navigate a project before they can build anything. This is the on-ramp for the level layout practical (Chunk 9).

**Worked example**

> Live demo: Open Epic Games Launcher → sign in → create a new Blank project with Starter Content → name it and choose a save location → click Create. Walk through each panel: Viewport (where the 3D world is), Content Browser (where all your files live), Details panel (where properties show up), Outliner (the hierarchy). Open a Starter Content folder, drag a material into the viewport. Point out that every panel is an abstraction hiding complexity — the Content Browser shows folders instead of raw file paths, the Details panel shows sliders instead of raw numbers.

**Guided practice + 80% checkpoint**

> Students follow the same create-a-project steps on their own machines. Mr. G circulates to troubleshoot login issues, driver errors, and missing Starter Content. Checkpoint: 18 of 22 students have a Blank project open with Starter Content visible in the Content Browser.

**Independent work**

> Students explore the Content Browser: find the Starter Content → Materials folder, drag three different materials onto objects in the viewport. Use the Outliner to select objects and see their properties in the Details panel.

**Exit ticket**

1. What are the four main panels in the Unreal Editor, and what does each one show you?
2. Why does the Content Browser show folders and thumbnails instead of raw file paths? (Connect to abstraction.)

**Exit ticket mastery criteria:** Correctly names at least three of the four panels and explains that the Content Browser is an abstraction that hides file system complexity from the user.

## Differentiation & Accommodations

- **Engagement:** First time opening a real game engine — the hook is the tool itself. Dragging materials onto 3D objects is immediately visual and satisfying.
- **Representation:** Live demo on projector mirrors what students see on their own screens. Editor panels are labeled on the projector before students look at their own monitors.
- **Action & Expression:** Exit ticket written or verbal. Students who finish early can try rotating and scaling objects in the viewport.
- **Accommodations:** Printed step list for project creation (start Launcher → sign in → Blank project → Starter Content → Create). Extended time. Pair-programming option for students struggling with login or navigation.
- **Student Voice:** Students choose which three materials to apply in independent practice.
