# GP1 - Devlog Checkpoint 02 (Day 32)

Day 32 (B day, 72 min) - Q1 Devlog Checkpoint. OBS screen recording of the ship game against the show-me checklist (through Video 15; Video 16 optional), plus reflection questions. A 15-minute fix buffer opens the block, and the early GitHub signup block rides today too.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|

## Do Now

| # | Type | Question |
|---|---------|----------|
| 1 | Review | Open your ship game and press play. Does the ship move with space, fly forward, and disappear the ring you hit? Name what works and what doesn't. |
| 2 | Review | What does your score display show when you hit a ring right now - the number ticking up, or nothing? If nothing, where does the chain break? |
| 3 | Preview | In one sentence: what is your game going to SHOW the viewer - what's the one thing you built that you're proudest of? |

## Submission

- OBS screen recording, per the posted setup instructions
- The recording walks the checklist below, in any order, one or more clips
- No narration today
- Reflection questions answered on the Classroom assignment

## What the recording needs to show (through Video 15)

- Show your ship imported and named appropriately (Video 11)
- Show the ship flying forward when you press play (Video 13) - double-evidence: proves Game Mode setup (11), Add Force (13), and movement in one line
- Show the spacebar impulse (bounce) working (Video 12)
- Show a ring disappearing when the ship overlaps it (Video 14) - double-evidence: overlap event + Cast + Destroy Actor
- Show the score ticking up when you score (Video 15) - double-evidence: Player Controller Score variable + Widget display + the wiring chain
- Show your Event Graph(s), scrolling slowly (Videos 12-15) - with Add Points functions visible if you did Video 16 (optional; no points lost without it)
- Show your customizations (what you chose - label text, font, colors, collider sizing, obstacle layout)

## Agenda (B day, 72 min)

1. Do Now review + pre-flight (5 min)
2. Fix buffer from the pre-flight answers (15 min): circulate with the checklist.
3. OBS recording against the checklist (25-30 min, circulating)
4. Early GitHub signup block (15 min): anyone without a school-work GitHub account makes one now, school email, verification email check while pending.
   - NOTE (do not skip): students use school email, no personal info on the account, no full name or birthday anywhere on the profile. The GitHub account is a work identity, not a personal one.
5. Submit recording + reflection questions on Classroom (7-10 min, runs into the bell if needed)
6. Exit ticket (5 min)

**~80% checkpoint (mid-build, replaces the standard one):** by 25 minutes in, every student has either a recording in progress or, if stuck, is paired with a working neighbor. Nobody is recording an empty checklist - if the pre-flight said "broken," they're in the fix buffer first.

## Exit ticket

1. Which checklist item were you proudest to show, and why? (This is the "what did you customize" line asked as reflection.)
2. In the recording, what is your game's STORY - what's the player trying to do, and how does the score tell them they did it?
3. What would you add or fix next if the quarter didn't end (one specific item)?
4. (If you made a GitHub account today:) did the verification email go through, and what username did you pick?

**Exit ticket mastery criteria:** Q1: names a specific item (a mechanic, a build detail, a visual choice) with a real reason. Q2: goal stated as fly-through-the-rings, score ticking as the progress signal, either phrased by the student or echoed from the checklist - real wording counts more than formal language. Q3: an actual item (a mechanic, a game-feel change, a UI idea) - "nothing" or "I don't know" doesn't count, and "add more rings" only counts if they say what would be different. Q4: yes with the username or another verifiable marker (verification email screenshot, profile screenshot) counts - proof, not the string.

## Differentiation & Accommodations

- **Engagement:** the devlog is their own game being shown and defended, not a quiz. The checklist credits what they chose (customizations) alongside what the unit required.
- **Representation:** date-based evidence, not pacing-based: students submit what exists today. OBS instructions are Nick's standing visual guide.
- **Action & Expression:** the medium IS expression - a recording, not paragraphs. Reflection questions give room for written or typed voices.
- **Accommodations:** the B day's 72 minutes plus the explicit fix buffer are the extended time; students who were still catching up Wednesday record what exists. Students without OBS experience pair with a peer mid-recording.
- **Student Voice:** reflection Q3 is deliberately open; the "show your customizations" checklist line is always student-chosen.
